# Interdisciplinary Frameworks

Cross-disciplinary concept mapping and migration guidelines.

## Purpose

When research spans multiple disciplines, concepts must be explicitly bridged with theoretical justification. This document provides frameworks for responsible cross-disciplinary integration.

## Concept Migration Protocol

### Steps

1. **Identify Source Discipline**: Where the concept originates
2. **Map to Target Discipline**: How it applies in the new context
3. **State Theoretical Justification**: Why the analogy holds
4. **Define Applicable Boundaries**: Where the analogy breaks down
5. **Provide Empirical Examples**: Real-world instances of the migration

### Template

```
Concept: [Name] ([Source Discipline]) → [Target Discipline]
Justification: [Why the analogy holds]
Boundary: [Where it stops being valid]
Example: [Concrete instance with citation]
```

## Validated Concept Migrations

### Physics → Social Sciences

| Concept | Source | Target | Justification | Key Boundary |
|---------|--------|--------|---------------|--------------|
| Entropy | Thermodynamics | Organizational Management | Both exhibit irreversible disorder increase | Only applies to closed systems without external restructuring |
| Phase Transition | Statistical Mechanics | Market Regime Change | Sudden collective behavior shift at critical thresholds | Markets have intentional agents; physical systems do not |
| Equilibrium | Classical Mechanics | Economic Equilibrium | State where opposing forces balance | Economic equilibria are contested; physical ones are measurable |

### Legal → Technology

| Concept | Source | Target | Justification | Key Boundary |
|---------|--------|--------|---------------|--------------|
| Trust (fiduciary) | Common Law | Blockchain Governance | Separation of legal title from beneficial use mirrors smart contract delegation | Trust law assumes human trustee with conscience; code has none |
| Jurisdiction | International Law | Digital Sovereignty | Authority over defined domain | Physical borders vs. network topology |
| Precedent | Case Law | Protocol Standards | Prior decisions constrain future behavior | Legal precedent allows distinguishing; code is deterministic |

### Economics → Other Fields

| Concept | Source | Target | Justification | Key Boundary |
|---------|--------|--------|---------------|--------------|
| Fama-French Three-Factor | Financial Economics | Venture Capital | Systematic risk factors explain return variation | VC returns are fat-tailed; public market models assume normality |
| Comparative Advantage | International Trade | Team Composition | Specialization increases total output | Team members cannot be traded like goods |
| Moral Hazard | Insurance Theory | Public Policy | Risk transfer changes behavior | Policy contexts involve political economy beyond individual incentives |

### Biology → Management

| Concept | Source | Target | Justification | Key Boundary |
|---------|--------|--------|---------------|--------------|
| Ecosystem | Ecology | Platform Business | Network of interdependent participants | Business ecosystems have intentional design; natural ones do not |
| Natural Selection | Evolutionary Biology | Market Competition | Differential survival based on fitness | Markets have regulation and bailouts; nature does not |
| Symbiosis | Ecology | Strategic Alliance | Mutual benefit from cooperation | Business alliances have exit options; biological symbiosis often does not |

## Cross-Disciplinary Integration Checklist

Before presenting a cross-disciplinary concept:

- [ ] Source concept accurately represented (not oversimplified)
- [ ] Target discipline framework acknowledged
- [ ] Theoretical basis for migration stated explicitly
- [ ] Scope and limitations clearly defined
- [ ] At least one empirical example provided
- [ ] Key scholars from both disciplines cited
- [ ] Known critiques of the migration acknowledged
- [ ] Alternative mappings considered and evaluated

## Anti-Patterns

### Over-Extension
Applying a concept beyond its justified scope without acknowledging limits.
**Example**: "Markets are Darwinian" → ignoring regulation, bailouts, and institutional design.

### False Precision
Implying quantitative exactness in qualitative analogies.
**Example**: Calculating organizational "entropy" to two decimal places.

### Source Amnesia
Using a migrated concept without citing its disciplinary origin.
**Example**: Using "network effects" without acknowledging Metcalfe's Law or economics literature.

### Authority Laundering
Using a prestigious source discipline to lend unwarranted authority to claims in the target discipline.
**Example**: Citing quantum mechanics to justify a management consulting framework.

## Disciplinary Authority Maps

When bridging disciplines, cite authorities from **both** sides:

| Bridge | Source Authority | Target Authority |
|--------|-----------------|------------------|
| Law → Blockchain | Maitland, trust law scholars | Buterin, Szabo, DeFi academic literature |
| Physics → Management | Boltzmann, Prigogine | Organizational theory journals (ASQ, AMJ) |
| Economics → VC | Fama, French, Sharpe | VC academic literature (Gompers, Lerner) |
| Biology → Platforms | Ecology textbooks, May | Platform economics (Rochet, Tirole) |
